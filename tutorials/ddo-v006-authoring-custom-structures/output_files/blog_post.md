# We Killed Our Daily Standup. Here's What Happened.

*By Priya Nakamura, Eng Manager | 2026.05.20*

---


Our daily standup used to eat 27 minutes a day for a 14-person team, and most of that time was spent waiting for your two minutes to talk. So four months ago, we killed it. Here's exactly what we replaced it with, and what happened next.


The firmware team grew from 4 people to 14 across Portland, Berlin, and Bangalore, and the 9:30am call only landed in normal working hours for 9 of them. The other 5 were dialing in before sunrise, after dinner, or just skipping the meeting outright.


So we swapped the live call for an async written update posted by 10am local time, plus a single `@blocked` tag that pings the on-call lead directly instead of waiting for tomorrow's meeting to surface a problem.


Six weeks in, standup-related meeting time dropped from about 27 minutes a day per person to roughly 6, and a flagged blocker now gets a first response in a median of 41 minutes instead of waiting up to a full day for the next standup. Twelve of fourteen engineers said they preferred the new format when we asked anonymously.


If your team's standup has outgrown its time slot, you don't need a fancy tool to fix it — just a shared channel and one clear rule for what happens when someone's blocked. Grab the exact async-update template we use and try it with your team for two weeks before you decide it's not for you.
