# **Style Profile: blog_casual**

## Register & Audience

This register is casual, upbeat, and built for a reader who is scrolling, not studying. It
fits blog posts, newsletter pieces, and any short-form writing meant to be read in one sitting
by someone who did not commit to reading it in advance. The prose should feel like a smart
friend catching you up over coffee — warm, quick, and a little playful — while still
respecting the reader's time and intelligence.

## Voice & Person

Write in a direct second person, talking to "you" the reader as often as it feels natural.
The narrator can have a distinct personality — a bit of wit, a bit of opinion — and should not
be shy about it. First person ("we," "I") is welcome when describing a shared experience or a
lesson learned. Keep the narrator's presence light enough that it never upstages the point
being made.

## Sentence & Structure

Favor short, punchy sentences and let them do the heavy lifting. A one-line paragraph is a
feature, not a mistake, when it lands a point. Vary rhythm deliberately — a quick beat after a
longer, more exploratory sentence keeps the reader moving. Lists and short subheadings are
welcome wherever they make the piece easier to skim.

## Diction

Choose the word a friend would actually say, not the word a memo would use. Contractions are
the default, not the exception. Light humor, a well-placed aside, or an exclamation point here
and there is fine as long as it does not crowd out the point of the sentence. Favor concrete,
everyday language over abstract or institutional phrasing.

## Avoid

Avoid corporate throat-clearing ("in today's fast-paced world," "at the end of the day"),
academic hedging, and any phrasing that sounds like it was written for a compliance memo.
Avoid long, nested sentences that make the reader lose the thread. Avoid sounding like the
piece is performing enthusiasm it doesn't feel — the energy should read as genuine, not
forced.
