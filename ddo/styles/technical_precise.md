# **Style Profile: technical\_precise**

## Register & Audience

This register is precise, technical, and unadorned, suited to a reader who is themselves an
expert or practitioner — a researcher, an engineer, a reviewer evaluating methodology. The
document should read like a specification or a technical report: exact, terse where
possible, and free of decoration that does not serve comprehension. Nothing about the tone
should soften or dramatize the underlying material; the writing should get out of the way of
the content it is describing.

## Voice & Person

Write in third person or in the impersonal passive where convention calls for it (as in
methods or procedure descriptions), reserving first-person plural ("we observed," "we
report") for statements of what the authors themselves did or concluded. Keep the narrator
almost invisible — the reader's attention should stay on the system, method, or result being
described rather than on the person describing it.

## Sentence & Structure

Favor short to medium declarative sentences with a single, unambiguous reading. Avoid
nested subordinate clauses that force the reader to hold multiple ideas in suspension.
Structure paragraphs so that each one advances a single technical point, and use consistent,
parallel phrasing across enumerated steps, lists, or comparable items so the structure itself
signals equivalence.

## Diction

Use precise, consistent terminology — once a term is introduced for a concept, use that same
term throughout rather than varying it for stylistic effect. Prefer concrete, literal
phrasing over metaphor or figurative language. Favor the active voice when describing what a
system or component does, and use the passive voice only where the actor is not the point of
the sentence.

## Avoid

Avoid rhetorical flourish, metaphor, and emotionally coded language. Avoid vague qualifiers
("somewhat," "fairly," "quite") in favor of exact, unambiguous phrasing. Avoid synonym
variation for terms that already have an established meaning in context. Avoid contractions
and colloquialisms, which read as imprecise in a technical register.
