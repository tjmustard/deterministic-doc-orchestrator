# **Style Profile: agenda\_directive**

## Register & Audience

This register is brisk, scannable, and directive, suited to a reader who will skim the
document in the minute before a meeting starts rather than read it closely. It fits meeting
agendas and any pre-read that exists to tell a busy attendee what is about to happen and what
is expected of them. The prose should feel like a set of clear instructions posted on a door,
not like a narrative account of the meeting to come.

## Voice & Person

Write in the imperative mood, addressing the reader directly as the person about to walk into
the room. Favor commands and direct statements of expectation ("Review the attached brief
first," "Bring a recommendation") over descriptions of what someone else will do. Keep the
narrator invisible — there is no storyteller here, only a set of directions.

## Sentence & Structure

Favor short, front-loaded clauses that put the action verb first, so a reader scanning down
the page can tell instantly what each line asks of them. Keep phrasing parallel across
consecutive items so the structure itself signals that they are the same kind of thing. Prefer
fragments and clipped phrasing over full narrative sentences wherever the meaning still lands
clearly.

## Diction

Prefer concrete action verbs — decide, review, approve, present, resolve — over vague
placeholders like "discuss" or "touch base" that could mean anything. Use plain, everyday
words over stiff or bureaucratic phrasing, and favor the active voice throughout so it is
always clear who is expected to act.

## Avoid

Avoid narrative or storytelling framing that describes the meeting instead of directing the
reader through it. Avoid the passive voice, which obscures who is responsible for an item.
Avoid vague filler phrases ("sync up," "touch base," "align on things") in favor of a specific
action. Avoid apologetic hedging ("just a quick," "if we have time") that undercuts the
directness the register depends on.
