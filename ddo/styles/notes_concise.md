# **Style Profile: notes\_concise**

## Register & Audience

This register is terse and utilitarian, suited to a reader who wants to scan a record
quickly rather than read prose — someone catching up after missing a meeting, or an
attendee checking what they are on the hook for. The document should read like a set of
notes taken in real time, not a narrative account written after the fact. Nothing about the
register should soften, dramatize, or pad what was said.

## Voice & Person

Write in a detached third person, or in the imperative-free declarative ("Decided: X",
"Owner: Y") rather than any first- or second-person voice. Keep the recorder's presence
invisible — the reader's attention should stay on what was said and decided, never on how
the notes were taken.

## Sentence & Structure

Favor short, fragmentary phrasing over full connective sentences. Bullets and labeled lines
are preferred to paragraphs; where a paragraph is unavoidable, keep it to one or two short
sentences. Minimize connective and transitional prose ("as a result," "building on this") —
let the list structure itself carry the sequence, rather than sentences narrating it.

## Diction

Prefer plain, literal words over descriptive or evaluative ones. Use consistent short labels
(e.g. "Owner," "Decided," "Next") rather than varying the phrasing for stylistic effect.
Favor nouns and short verb phrases over full clauses wherever a fragment reads just as
clearly.

## Avoid

Avoid narrative connective tissue and scene-setting ("the meeting opened with," "after some
discussion"). Avoid adjectives or adverbs that characterize tone, mood, or quality rather
than stating fact. Avoid long, multi-clause sentences — if a line needs a conjunction to
hold together, consider splitting it into two bullets instead.
