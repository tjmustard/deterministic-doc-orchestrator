# **Style Profile: formal\_professional**

## Register & Audience

This register is formal, measured, and professional. It suits readers who expect a document
to behave like an institutional artifact — an engineering manager, an executive sponsor, a
compliance reviewer — rather than a casual memo. The prose should feel composed and
deliberate, as if every sentence survived a review pass before it reached the page.
Warmth is acceptable, but it is understated; the reader should sense competence and
restraint before they sense personality.

## Voice & Person

Write in third person or in a neutral, de-personalized first-person plural ("we recommend")
when a document must refer to its own authorship. Avoid addressing the reader directly with
"you" except in explicit instructions or acceptance-criteria language, where directness aids
clarity. Keep the narrator's presence quiet — the document's structure and evidence should
carry the authority, not the narrator's opinions.

## Sentence & Structure

Favor complete, well-formed sentences over fragments. Vary sentence length for rhythm, but
lean toward medium-length sentences with a single clear clause structure over long, nested
constructions. Paragraphs should open with a topic sentence and stay tightly scoped to one
idea. Prefer parallel structure in lists and headings so the document reads as a coherent
system rather than a loose collection of notes.

## Diction

Prefer precise, domain-appropriate vocabulary over colloquial substitutes. Use full words
rather than abbreviations or slang. Favor the active voice for describing system or process
behavior, and reserve the passive voice for cases where the actor is genuinely unknown or
irrelevant to the point being made. Keep word choice consistent across a document — once a
term is chosen for a concept, do not swap in a synonym later.

## Avoid

Avoid contractions, exclamation points, and rhetorical questions. Avoid casual interjections
("honestly," "basically," "just") and hedging filler ("kind of," "sort of," "probably").
Avoid humor or asides that would feel out of place in a formal review setting. Avoid
addressing the reader with imperative commands outside of clearly delineated instructional
or procedural sections.
