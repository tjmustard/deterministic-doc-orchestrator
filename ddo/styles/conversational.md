# **Style Profile: conversational**

## Register & Audience

This register is casual and approachable, suited to a reader who wants the document to feel
like a knowledgeable colleague talking them through something rather than a formal report.
It fits internal notes, blog-style writeups, and any audience that would find a stiff,
institutional tone distancing. The prose should feel relaxed without becoming sloppy —
friendly, but still trustworthy.

## Voice & Person

Write in a direct, personable first or second person. Address the reader as "you" when it
helps the writing feel like a conversation rather than a lecture. It is fine for the
narrator's presence to show through — a light aside or a moment of enthusiasm is welcome as
long as it does not crowd out the substance of the sentence it sits in.

## Sentence & Structure

Mix shorter and longer sentences freely, and let occasional fragments stand on their own for
emphasis. Paragraphs can be short — sometimes just a sentence or two — if that keeps the
pacing brisk. Favor a natural, spoken rhythm over rigid, symmetrical structure; it is fine
for a list to have some looseness in how each item is phrased.

## Diction

Prefer everyday words over formal or technical substitutes when both would work equally
well. Contractions are welcome and generally preferred over their expanded forms. Favor the
active voice and simple, familiar verbs over stiff or bureaucratic phrasing.

## Avoid

Avoid stiff, formal phrasing, dense jargon, and bureaucratic hedging ("it should be noted
that," "in the event that"). Avoid overly long, multi-clause sentences that would slow a
reader down. Avoid sounding cold or distant — the tone should feel like it is speaking with
the reader, not at them.
